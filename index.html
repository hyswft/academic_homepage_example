About

I use first-principles calculations and machine learning to understand and design materials for energy storage and conversion. My group develops methods that connect quantum-mechanical simulations to the length and time scales where real devices fail or succeed.

Before joining University Name, I was a postdoctoral researcher at National Laboratory and received my PhD in Materials Science from Another University.

Research

Ion transport in solid electrolytes

Density functional theory and ab initio molecular dynamics to find the structural features that enable fast lithium and sodium conduction.

Machine-learned interatomic potentials

Building accurate, transferable potentials that reach million-atom simulations of interfaces, grain boundaries and defects.

High-throughput materials discovery

Automated workflows that screen thousands of candidate compounds for stability, synthesizability and target properties.
